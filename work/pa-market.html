Group 01

Financial Planning & Cost Control

Models built to give leadership a forward-looking, assumption-driven view of financial performance — turning "should we do this?" and "where is margin leaking?" into structured, testable questions.

Lunch Initiative — 18-Month Financial Projection Model

In active use
Problem

Lunch service was significantly underperforming relative to other dayparts. Leadership needed a structured way to evaluate whether — and how — the lunch business could be revived, rather than relying on gut instinct.

Approach

Built an 18-month, assumption-driven projection model to map a credible month-by-month recovery path, rather than a single end-state estimate — so leadership could see how the business was expected to ramp, not just where it might land.

How it works Draft — pending detail
  • Historical lunch daypart sales, covers, and average check as the baseline
  • Three scenarios (conservative / base / aggressive) built around traffic recovery rate, pricing, and marketing push
  • Monthly ramp curve tying labor and food cost assumptions to projected covers, rather than holding them flat
  • Sensitivity toggles so leadership could stress-test the plan against slower-than-expected recovery
What was built

A spreadsheet-based financial model with a linked assumptions tab and monthly P&L projection, plus a one-page PDF summary for leadership review.

Outcome Draft — pending real figures

Model is actively used to track lunch performance against the 18-month plan; early tracking is being monitored against the base-case scenario as the initiative progresses.

Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) Model

In active use
Problem Draft — pending detail

With three distinct revenue lines — restaurant, catering, and events — each carrying different recipe costs and vendor pricing, leadership lacked a clear, consistent view of true food and beverage cost as a percentage of revenue by line and by location.

Approach Draft — pending detail

Built a model to calculate theoretical COGS from recipe-level ingredient costs, compare it against actual purchasing and usage data, and surface variance so cost leakage could be caught early rather than discovered at month-end close.

How it works Draft — pending detail
  • Recipe-level ingredient costing rolled up to a theoretical COGS % per revenue line
  • Actual vendor invoice costs compared against theoretical to flag variance
  • Breakout by location and by revenue stream (restaurant / catering / events) rather than one blended number
What was built

A spreadsheet-based COGS tracking model with a monthly variance report used in operational reviews.

Outcome Draft — pending real figures

Gave leadership visibility into COGS variance by location and revenue line for the first time, surfacing where actual cost was drifting from recipe-based expectations.

Inventory & Pricing Intelligence

Inventory Analytics

In active use
Problem Draft — pending detail

Approach Draft — pending detail

How it works Draft — pending detail
  • Usage trend tracking by item and location against set par levels

  • Location-level comparison to separate one-off issues from systemic ones

Outcome Draft — pending real figures

In active use

Group 03

Workforce Optimization

A model built to align labor with actual demand across dayparts, locations, and service lines — the largest controllable cost in a hospitality operation.

Staff Scheduling Model

In active use
Problem Draft — pending detail

Labor scheduling across dayparts and locations wasn't closely tied to actual demand patterns, creating a mix of overstaffing during slow periods and understaffing during peak periods.

Approach Draft — pending detail

Built a demand-based scheduling model that ties staffing levels to sales and traffic forecasts by daypart, rather than scheduling off a fixed, historical template.

How it works Draft — pending detail
  • Historical sales and traffic patterns by daypart and day-of-week as the demand baseline
  • Labor hours recommended per shift based on projected demand, not a static template
  • Labor cost as a percentage of projected sales tracked against target by location
What was built

A spreadsheet-based scheduling and labor-cost model used to guide weekly schedule building.

Outcome Draft — pending real figures

Improved alignment between scheduled labor hours and actual demand, supporting better labor cost management across dayparts.
